Fine-tuning vs. RAG vs. prompting

Three answers to one wish — “make it better at my task”. They are not competitors, and the expensive one is almost never the one you needed.

DOOR 1

Prompting

Changes
the words you send
Cost
nothing
New info live
instantly
Good at
tone, length, format, one-off tasks
Can’t do
supply facts it was never given
DOOR 2

Retrieval (RAG)

Changes
what gets pasted in before your question
Cost
low, ongoing
New info live
as soon as the document is indexed
Good at
your facts, your files, anything that changes
Can’t do
change how it writes, or fix a bad retrieval
DOOR 3

Fine-tuning

Changes
the model’s own numbers — its “weights,” the parameters from page 3
Cost
high, and again for every update
New info live
weeks — a whole training run
Good at
a consistent style or format, at scale
Can’t do
reliably store facts you need to be correct

Pick what you actually need

The expensive mistake

Fine-tuning does not teach facts

Trained on your handbook, it gets fluent at sounding like your handbook — including confident sentences about policies you never had. Facts that must be right belong on the desk.

Start at door 1 because it is free, and move to door 2 the moment the answer depends on something the model cannot know. Door 3 is for when both already work and you need that behaviour thousands of times a day.

So what

Ask which thing is wrong: the instructions, the information, or the manner. Each has its own door, and only one of them is expensive.
